# Redefining the Global Target Account List: Using Data to Focus Marketing Spend

**Summary:** I led a comprehensive analysis to evaluate the effectiveness of the company's Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) model and helped marketing leadership redefine the Global Target Account List (TAL). The work resulted in a highly targeted list that reduced account volume by over 50% while retaining 94% of the revenue potential. 

**My role:** I owned the end-to-end analysis, from defining the methodology and structuring the data workflows to synthesizing the findings and presenting the final strategic recommendation to stakeholders.

**Project status:** Analysis delivered, and the new TALs are now in use.

**Public evidence:** Fictionalized demonstration data.

## The problem:
The company's marketing campaigns and performance teams were targeting 98.4% of the total account universe. Leadership needed to know if targeting the highest ICP grades (A and B) was actually driving more pipeline, or if the TAL should be built around industry and revenue bands instead. With nearly every account being targeted, budget and effort were spread too thin. They needed a data-backed decision on what truly drives performance to build a leaner, more effective Global TAL. 

## My approach and key decisions
* **Segmenting customers vs. prospects:** I explicitly separated existing paying customers from pure prospects during the pipeline analysis. I made this decision because customer accounts carry significantly more pipeline, and mixing them together would skew the understanding of what makes a good acquisition target. 
* **Using a lift index instead of totals or conversion rates:** I used a lift index (a segment's share of pipeline divided by its share of accounts) instead of totals or conversion rates. Totals mislead because grades differ in size. The index answers the targeting question directly ("are these accounts better than average?"). An index of 1.0 means the segment performs exactly like a random pick of accounts.
* **Testing assumptions about industry:** I challenged the assumption that industry vertical was a primary driver of performance. I investigated the engagement rates across different revenue bands within each industry to see if the industry itself mattered, or if it was simply a proxy for company size. 
* **Letting size decide who is in, and grade decide how much each account gets:** Crossing grade with size showed both carried signal, but not in the same places. Size lifted engagement in every grade with no exceptions. Grade separated accounts strongly above $500M and barely at all below $50M.
 
## Findings 
* The ICP model was highly effective, but its predictive power was heavily concentrated among large companies. Grade A accounts produced over 3 times the pipeline expected for their size, but below the $50M revenue mark, the ICP grade offered almost no meaningful signal. 
* I discovered that while industry initially appeared predictive, it was largely just a proxy for company size. The true driver of performance was the revenue band.
 
## Business Implication 
I proposed a new, highly focused Global TAL consisting of 4 distinct segments: transacting customers, unpenetrated accounts inside customer hierarchies, all prospects above $500M, and Grade A prospects down to $50M. 
Customers with their own subscription receive retention messaging. Accounts inside a paying parent with no subscription receive cross-sell messaging. All prospects above $500M receive acquisition messaging, with grades A and B getting the most investment. Grade A prospects from $50M to $500M receive acquisition messaging through lower-cost channels.

## Validation and limitations
* I validated the timeframe by restricting the activity metrics to only include data generated after the ICP model was released, ensuring we were measuring the correct model. 
* I noted a specific limitation: the ICP grades in the system represented the current state only, with no historical tracking. I communicated to stakeholders that while we could see strong grades and strong performance appearing together, we could not be 100% certain the grade caused the outcome, as an account might have been upgraded after creating pipeline. 

## Outcome
* **What I delivered:** A data-driven recommendation for a consolidated Global TAL, complete with a two-tiered investment strategy based on account grade and size. 
* **What improvement was measured:** The proposed targeting model reduced the overall list size by more than half, dropping low-performing prospect accounts. 
* **Business Impact:** Despite removing over 50% of the accounts, the new list successfully retained 91% of total marketing engagement, 93.9% of the pipeline, and 99.8% of closed-won revenue. 

## What I learned 
Analyzing high-level averages can hide critical business realities. Initially, Grade B accounts appeared to perform at exactly the baseline average. However, when I broke this grade down by company size, I found that Grade B accounts above $500M produced nearly 100 times more pipeline per account than those under $50M. This reinforced that a category cannot simply be ruled "in or out", it must be segmented to find the hidden value.
